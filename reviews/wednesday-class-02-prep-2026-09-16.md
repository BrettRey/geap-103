# Wednesday class 2: teaching prep

**September 16, 2026 · GEAP 103 · Getting AI to Do What I Mean**

This is the short plan for tomorrow's classroom pack. I'm assuming the scheduled 165 minutes, including a 15-minute break. I haven't assumed that everyone finished their folders or Goal Document last week.

## What students should leave with

- One request they tried, a check of the result, and a reason to accept, change, or reject it.
- A first Decision Log entry saved in their course folder.
- A short update to their wishes and practice explaining what they expected and what happened.

Students can use an available large language model (LLM), such as Copilot or ChatGPT. If access fails after one quick attempt, they work from prepared answers. No installation or new account is needed to participate.

## The sequence

| Elapsed time | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Reconnect with one wish from last week. Introduce the five vocabulary entries with brief examples. If someone has no wish saved, let them write one now. |
| 0:15–0:40 | Demonstrate a request, a restatement check, a revision, and a check against the source. Let students judge the answers with you. Show how this becomes a Decision Log. |
| 0:40–1:10 | Students choose a wish, make a request, inspect the answer, and revise or explain why they would keep it. Circulate and help them name a specific result they want. |
| 1:10–1:25 | Brief persona example, then pairs compare answers to the same prompt. Both partners explain which answer better meets the request and why. |
| 1:25–1:35 | Complete a class Decision Log together. Students save their prompts and answers before the break. |
| 1:35–1:50 | Break. |
| 1:50–2:10 | Students write their own first Decision Log. Protect this time if earlier activities run long. |
| 2:10–2:27 | Update the Goal Document and talk with a partner about an idea that became clearer or changed. |
| 2:27–2:45 | Rehearse, explain the interaction, answer follow-up questions, and save a one-sentence reflection. Close with next week's version-history preparation. |

## What I'll prepare

1. **Projection slides with teaching notes**, using the Day 1 visual style and short student-facing language.
2. **A demo ready to use**, with the source text, exact prompts, saved responses, and a fact students can check directly. A live response can differ from the saved one.
3. **A printable backup exercise**, with the source and two answers, space to check them, and a prompt-revision task. The teacher's copy will include the checks and answer provenance.
4. **A teacher's checklist and the existing Decision Log template**, together in one folder. No posting to Blackboard or GitHub without your instruction.

## Two points to keep accurate

A clearer request can help, but it doesn't guarantee a better answer. A poor answer isn't automatically the student's fault. If the first answer works, students can check it and justify keeping it.

An LLM writing as an expert is still producing an LLM answer. A change of voice, or agreement between two answers, doesn't verify a fact. Students should check one concrete detail against the source or a direct test.
