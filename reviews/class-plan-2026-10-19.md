## 2. Milling: talk the decisions through (30 minutes)

Use `materials/milling-2026-10-19-projector.html`. Give students different starting numbers. Students with their own project answer the same questions about it.

- **0:10–0:12.** Students read the questions and choose where to start.
- **0:12–0:32.** Mingle. Signal a partner change about every three minutes, for about six conversations. Ask them to use at least four different questions. Listen to questions 5 and 6. They lead into the demonstration.
- **0:32–0:40.** Back together. Take two or three answers to question 5 ("When you get an answer wrong, what should an app do?") and question 6 ("What could go wrong if AI writes phrases in your language?"). Point out that the answers differ, and that each one is a decision someone will make for their own app.

## 3. Demonstration: deciding with the agent (20 minutes)

**What it shows.** How to make a decision with the agent and record it, not what to build. Stop before the agent builds anything. Whatever appears on the projector becomes the model students copy.

**Phrases.** Ask a volunteer for four short phrases in their language, with English meanings, and have them write the phrases on the board. If nobody volunteers, use four English phrases and say who the learner is.

**Step 1: ask for ideas, not an app (about 8 minutes).** In the `demo-app` project, type the "Ideas first" prompt from "Prompts to display" at the end of this plan. Read the answer aloud. Ask the class: "Which idea would be best for this learner? Why?" Take two answers.

**Step 2: choose out loud (about 3 minutes).** Choose one idea and say why, including what you're giving up: "I'll take idea 2, because … Idea 1 would be … , but …"

**Step 3: write the Decision Log entry live (about 7 minutes).** Open `decision-log.md` and fill in the fields briefly, saying how each one matches what just happened:

| Decision Log field | Today |
|---|---|
| Task goal and constraints | Who the app is for, the situation, the three rules |
| Tool used | The agent in Copilot, and the model |
| Prompt snippets | The "Ideas first" prompt, and later "The smallest version" |
| Output summary | The three ideas, or later the first version |
| Decision | Which idea you accepted, changed or rejected, and why |
| Verification | Checking every phrase against your handwritten list, and trying it as your learner would |
| What changed | What you changed after checking |

Say: "This is graded work. Eight entries are a quarter of your mark, and your app will give you plenty to write about."

**Step 4: show the build prompt without running it (about 2 minutes).** Display "The smallest version" from "Prompts to display" and say: "After you choose, this is the prompt you'll run yourself, today or next class. When the page opens, check every phrase against your list before anything else."

**Teacher note.** Building the first version on screen would show what "smallest version" means, but students would copy its layout, quiz style and colours. If students seem lost in work time, build one for a single group at their table instead of on the projector.

**If Copilot fails live,** do step 1 in a chat interface by pasting the phrases. The chat interface can give ideas but can't make files, which is a useful contrast.

## 4. Choose, write by hand, make a folder (15 minutes)

1. **Alone or in a group.** Groups of up to three sit together. Each person keeps their own folder and Decision Log for now, and the group compares ideas and, later, versions. A shared repository comes with Tasks 7–11. Before a group builds anything, they agree who is responsible for each part (Task 24, step 4). Each person must be able to show and explain their own part.
2. **Write by hand, no AI.** Write who the app is for, the first situation, and four phrases with their meanings. Students with their own project write who it's for and four facts, rules or examples it needs. This list is what they'll check the agent's work against.
3. **Make the folder.** Students ask the agent to make a folder for the app inside `GEAP103` in the home folder (for example `GEAP103/language-app`), then add it as a project in Copilot. Not OneDrive, Desktop or Documents. Git and the portfolio come when they reach Tasks 7 and 8.

Early finishers write the ideas prompt in their own words and compare with a partner.

## 5. Work time and conferences (60 minutes)

**Today's goal is a choice, not an app.** The project runs until the final conferences in December. First versions come over the next classes, and some students will get there sooner than others.

**Steps for students.** Write these on the board:

1. Ask the agent for three ideas. Don't build yet.
2. Choose one, and be ready to say why.
3. Start your Decision Log entry about the choice.

If you get there:

4. Ask for the smallest version.
5. Open it. Check every phrase against your handwritten list. Try it as your learner would.

Students read each permission request before approving it, as in Task 1. Students still on Tasks 1–3 are practising the same skills (controlling Copilot, making something from notes), so they can join in with help from a partner. Students who get a first version today can do Task 4 with it: make a copy before a change, then bring it back. That gives them something to show at a conference.

The Decision Log entry is about 150–200 words. Students start it today and finish it before November 2.

**No Copilot.** Some students may be without Copilot or GitHub. They do the same steps on paper: sketch the first screen, and ask a chat interface for the three ideas by pasting their phrases. Their Decision Log entry is about that choice, and they build the app at home or next class. Ask them to tell you which step is blocked.

**Conferences.** Three Monday students had conferences on October 5, and seventeen haven't. At five minutes each, about 45 minutes fits nine students, and the rest of the time goes to helping.

- **Who goes first:** ask "Who's ready to show Task 3 or Task 4?" Their October 10 messages asked for that, and the messages file tells you what each student was asked to show.
- **Today's work counts too.** A student can show the three ideas, the one they chose, and why. That's evidence for guiding AI and evaluating AI text.
- **Every conference:** ask "What did you ask the AI? What did you keep, change or reject, and why?"
- **Logging:** log in the tracker as before (Log sheet: student, Conference, up to three outcomes with levels, Y under R, T or P for any required check shown).

The rest go on November 2 and after.

## 6. Explain your choice to a partner, and close (10 minutes)

- **2:30–2:36.** Pairs, not from the same group, take turns explaining their choice: "My app is for … . It teaches … . I chose … because … . I didn't choose … because … ." The partner asks one question about it. This is practice for explaining technical work in a conference.
- **2:36–2:40.** Each student writes one sentence on paper and hands it in: "My app is for … . It teaches … . I chose … because … ." Students with their own project write: "My project is … . My first version will … ." This also tells you who has chosen their own project.

**Remind them:**

- Decision Log feedback round 1 comes back on November 2. Today's entry should be finished by then.

## Prompts to display

**Ideas first:**

> I want to make a small web app that teaches [my language] to [who it's for]. Here are 4 phrases and their meanings: [phrases]. Don't build anything yet. Give me three different ideas for what the learner could do with these phrases. Use simple English. For each idea, give one good point and one problem.

**The smallest version:**

> Make the smallest version of idea [number] as one web page in this folder. Use only my 4 phrases, exactly as I wrote them. Don't add other phrases or features. Then tell me how to open it.

**One change at a time:**

> Change only this: [one change]. Don't change anything else. Then tell me what you changed.

**Board frames:** "I chose … because …", "I didn't choose … because …", "I checked … against …", "The agent changed …", "My learner needs …"
