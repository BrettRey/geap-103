Each student keeps one card and asks several classmates the same main question, so answers become easier to compare and the language gets repeated practice.

Each card has one main question, an optional follow-up, and a short answer starter. The shared instruction is: **Ask. Listen. Answer their question. Find a new partner. You can pass on a question.**
### Card 1
**What do you use your phone or computer for outside class?**

Ask more: Which thing do you do most often?

Start: I use it to…
### Card 2
**How do you like to learn something new?**

Ask more: Can you give an example?

Start: I like to learn by…
### Card 3
**What's something you enjoy doing or know a lot about?**

Ask more: How did you get interested in it?

Start: I enjoy… / I know a lot about…
### Card 4
**What's something you'd like to learn to do this year?**

Ask more: Why would you like to learn that?

Start: I'd like to learn to…
### Card 5
**What's one everyday thing you wish were easier?**

Ask more: Who else might want that to be easier?

Start: I wish it were easier to…
### Card 6
**If you could make something with a computer, what would you like to make?**

Ask more: Who would use it or enjoy it?

Start: I'd like to make… / I'm not sure yet, but I like…
## Running the activity
Allow about 20 minutes. Give students a little time to read their card and think of their own answer. Model a short exchange. Let them ask for a different card. Students can stay seated while partners visit them. With 25 students, one group can have three people; make sure all three ask and answer.

After several conversations, ask: **“What did you have in common?” “What was different?” “Did you hear an idea you'd like to explore?”** Helpful starters: **“We both…” “Some people…, but others…” “Now I'd like to try…”** There doesn't have to be a match. Don't ask students to collect names, record conversations, or report someone else's personal story. Leave device access and individual support to the private form.

Use the form to arrange access support, choose short demonstrations, and identify interests for project exploration. Treat the skill questions as self-reports, not proof of what students can or can't do. Keep raw responses and any identifying exports in Humber systems, out of the public course repository and external LLMs. Share broad class patterns only when individuals can't be identified.
