## Monday: how it will go, and what to say

### At 8:55: the students who come on time, two weeks after they last opened their apps

Say once: "Open your laptop and your app. Everything's on the screen." Then start a conference with whoever already has an app open, rather than fixing someone's setup.

- **"Which app? Where is it?"** Point to "Open your app". Students click their old chat in the list on the left and type "Open my app in the browser. Don't make a new app." The agent in that chat knows where the app is, even when the student doesn't.
- **Why the old chat.** On your account, each new chat in Copilot started in its own new folder on its own branch, so a new chat doesn't contain the app. Copilot opens on a new chat, and a student who skims will type into it. In a test tonight (Copilot's command-line version on Auto, an empty folder), "Open my app in the browser" didn't build anything: the agent said the workspace was empty and asked where the app was. A B1 student won't know what that means, so the slide's next line says it: "Copilot says the folder is empty? You're in a new chat. Click your old chat." In a live chat the agent may offer to build one instead, and a student may accept; "Don't make a new app" is there for that.
- **"Copilot wants an update" or "I can't sign in."** The post asked them to do this last night. Some won't have. Updates take a few minutes; sign-in failures go on the "Need help" list.
- **"I don't have an app" or "I wasn't here last time."** Point to "No app?" The sentence is on the slide, with a gap for the language. A build took about two minutes in the test on your account; a student's plan may be slower or out of requests. "Can't build?" sends them to the "Need help" list.
- **A group member whose app is on someone else's laptop.** The slide says to sit together at the laptop where it was made. If that student is absent, decide whether the others may build their own app today, and say it aloud so the whole group hears the same answer.

### While students arrive: conferences

**The conference.**
- First: "Scroll up. Show me your first message." It confirms you're looking at the app they made, not a new one, and it gives the AI question something to point at.
- Then: "Show me your app. Teach me one thing in your language." Use the app, say the word or phrase aloud, and let the student correct you.
- Ask the v7 question: "What did you ask the AI? What did you keep, change or reject, and why?" Don't drop it when time is short. Outcomes 3 and 4 are pass-gate outcomes, and the framework lists in-class writing only for 2 and 5, so this question is the main way students show 3 and 4 without AI.
- Your one follow-up: "Did your app teach me that, or did you?"
- **End with their next step,** said aloud: "Next, fix the lessons that don't open," or, for a student you saw at 8:55 before they'd checked anything, "Next, do the checks on the screen. I'll ask to see your page next time." Write it in the tracker note, and open their next conference with "Show me what you did since last time."
- Neighbours will overhear. That's useful: the questions spread without your repeating them.

**Variations,** each in place of the usual follow-up:
- A student on their own project: play the person it's for.
- A Spanish speaker: you're learning Latin American Spanish, so ask which variety they speak.
- A student in a group: ask about the part they did. If someone else did all the prompting, ask what this student decided or changed.
- A language the student's computer has no voice for: the speaker button will be silent or use the wrong voice, so the student has to say the words. Your Mac has no voice for any African language; students' computers may differ.

**The tracker row** (Source: Conference). Record what the student showed, up to three outcomes:
- 6, the product, judged against "college standards, including basic accessibility features";
- 4, what the app left out or got wrong for you as its learner;
- 5, explaining their choices;
- 3, from the AI question.

### What the others will say while you're at someone's desk

- **"Teacher!" from across the room.** "Write your name on the board. I'll come." Work down the lists between conferences, alternating between them. Help calls are long in this room, so give each one about two minutes. If it isn't fixed by then, the student writes down what happened and what they tried, works on something else, and you come back after the next conference. That written description is the evidence for outcome 2.
- **"What do we do?"** Point at the slide. If they don't understand it, it says what to do: take a photo and ask a chatbot on their phone to explain it in their language. Some students already work through a chatbot this way.
- **"I asked for everything" or "I don't remember what I asked."** "Scroll up to your first message in that chat."
- **"Everything works,"** after a glance at the first screen. "How many lessons does it say? Open every one." In two of the three test builds, most of the advertised lessons didn't exist. The checks came from those builds, though, and a student's app may pass all of them. Then "Nothing to fix? Write why your app is good enough for a beginner" is the task.
- **"Can I write it in my language?"** "In English. Ask me for a word if you need one."
- **"I'm done,"** after ten minutes. Point at "Fix one problem": fix another. Each fix makes a new version, and the new version needs checking.
- **"The fix broke it."** "Ask Copilot to bring back the copy you saved." A student who can show that has the required check for outcome 1, a recovered earlier version. Log it if they show you.
- **A student whose language has no voice on their computer.** The slide says to write that down. Don't let them spend the class on it. They should fix something else.
- **Questions about Decision Log feedback,** if round 1 has gone out: "Bring it to your conference."
- **Students with no laptop:** they can sit with a classmate and do the checks on that app, and get their own conference next time.

### Troubleshooting

When a student on the "Need help" list gets you, ask first: "What did you want, and what happened?" An answer clear enough to act on is evidence for outcome 2, and often 5. If you log the call as a conference, it also meets the required check "a technical problem solved or described clearly enough to get help". Whether such a call counts as one of a student's conferences is your decision. It fits the v7 description: the student shows something, explains it, and gets one follow-up.

This won't make troubleshooting faster; it turns time you already spend into evidence. What may save time is the folder problem above. When work has vanished, the student is almost always in a new chat, so find the old one in the list on the left. If the old chat is gone, ask the agent to run `git worktree list`. On a Git project it lists every chat's folder on that computer; on your machine it showed all four of your GEAP 103 sessions.

### What you can't fix on the day

Some students will sit anyway, and some will leave after their conference. The pressure comes later, when the next conference asks to see their paper and their next step. Students who aren't focused will drift from the list. The whiteboard and the end-of-conference step are the parts that reach them, because they're about the student's own app.

## What's uncertain, and how you'll know it isn't working

**Uncertain.**
- Whether students' Copilot apps behave like yours (a new folder and branch for each chat, the default model). On Auto, yours picked GPT-6 Luna or Claude Sonnet 5.5.
- Whether a new chat in the desktop app answers "Open my app" the way the command-line test did (folder empty, nothing built), or offers to build one.
- Whether students' Copilot plans build as quickly as your account did, and whether any have run out of requests.
- Laptops or lab machines. That decides which voices the apps have, and whether "open the chat where you made it" works at all on a shared machine.
- Which languages are in each section.

**Signs it isn't working.**
- The student takes the mouse and teaches you in seconds every time, so the conference becomes a demonstration with no explanation.
- Conferences run to ten minutes because they're enjoyable, and you reach four students instead of eight.
- The "Need help" list never empties.
- The papers say "Alex" and "locked lessons" and nothing from students' own apps.
- At the end, you've logged fewer than six conferences.
