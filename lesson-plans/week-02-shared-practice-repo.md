# GEAP 103 Week 2 Lesson Plan: Shared Practice Repo Variant

**Context:** 3 x 55 min (150 min instruction + 15-min break), B1 learners, BYOD, Office 365 + any available AI tool  
**Week 2 of 14** | Phase 1: Discovery  
**Use with:** `materials/Week_02_Shared_Practice_Repo_Materials.md` and the public repo kit at `https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103-shared-practice`

**Why this variant exists:** The original Week 2 lesson asks students to begin from their own wishes. That remains pedagogically valuable, but it also creates wide variation in task difficulty, setup friction, and AI-credit use. This additive variant keeps the same CLOs and Decision Log work while giving the whole class the same inspectable files, the same common tasks, and a clearer no-credit fallback path.

**Learning Objectives (Week 2):**
1. Articulate a concrete task clearly enough for AI to act on it (CLO 3 begins)
2. Recognize when AI output does not match stated constraints and identify the gap (CLO 4 begins)
3. Document prompt revision, verification, and decision-making in a first Decision Log entry
4. Connect a common class task back to a personal interest in the Goal Document

**Design move:** Standardize the practice task, not the student's thinking. Students all begin with the same small files from a shared public repository. Their Goal Document still asks what interests them and what feels possible. The common repo reduces noise so the teacher can guide language, comparison, and verification more directly.

---

## Shared repo structure

Students download or open one public repository:

- `practice-files/01-study-spaces-article.md`
- `practice-files/02-club-message-notes.txt`
- `practice-files/03-snack-budget.csv`
- `prepared-outputs/04-prompt.md`
- `prepared-outputs/04-output-a.md`
- `prepared-outputs/04-output-b.md`

**Core route:** everyone starts with Task 1, then moves to Task 2 if ready.  
**Extension route:** Task 3 for faster students.  
**Fallback route:** Task 4 when live AI access is blocked or when the teacher wants a zero-credit comparison task.

## Segment A: Vocabulary + Worked Example (0:00--0:40)

### 0:00--0:05 | Warm-Up: Same Task, Different Results
**Format:** Whole class, interactive

- "Last week you wrote about what you want. Today we're going to try common files, so everyone can compare the same kind of task."
- Quick show of hands: "Who has used ChatGPT, Copilot, or another AI tool before? Did it do exactly what you wanted?"
- Listen for mismatch stories. Name the theme: the issue is often not only the tool, but what the person actually said.

### 0:05--0:15 | Vocabulary Pre-Training
**Format:** Instructor-led, interactive  
**Language skills:** Academic vocabulary building

**5 terms:** prompt, generate, revise, accept/reject, verification

Define each in plain language and point to where it appears in today's shared tasks.

- **prompt:** what you tell AI to do
- **generate:** the output AI creates
- **revise:** changing your request because the result was not right yet
- **accept / reject / modify:** deciding what to keep, throw away, or change
- **verification:** checking whether the result really matches the file and task

### 0:15--0:35 | Worked Example with the Shared Repo
**Format:** Instructor demonstration with think-aloud  
**Language skills:** Listening, critical evaluation vocabulary

Instructor opens `practice-files/01-study-spaces-article.md`.

**Step 1 -- Vague prompt:**
- Example: "Summarize this file."
- Output appears but is too general or ignores audience/format.
- Think aloud: "I knew what I wanted, but I didn't say enough. I didn't name the reader, length, or format."

**Step 1.5 -- Restatement check:**
- Ask: "Before you do anything, tell me what you think I'm asking for."
- Show how the AI's restatement exposes missing constraints.

**Step 2 -- Revised prompt:**
- Example: "Read `practice-files/01-study-spaces-article.md`. Write 6 short bullet points for a new B1 English student. Keep the opening times, the food rule, and the charger rule. End with one recommendation."
- Better output appears.
- Verify 2-3 details against the file.

**Step 3 -- Decision language on projector:**
- What did I mean?
- What did I say?
- What did I get?
- What changed when I revised?
- What did I verify?

### 0:35--0:40 | Connect to Guided Practice
**Format:** Instructor-led

- "Now you'll all begin with the same first file. That means we can compare results more easily."
- "Later, you'll still connect this back to your own interests in the Goal Document. The common file is just the practice surface."

## Segment B: Planning + Guided Practice (0:40--1:35)

### 0:40--0:47 | Pre-Task Planning
**Format:** Individual reflection

Students open the shared repo and Task 1 in `TASKS.md`.

- "Before you prompt, read the file and the task."
- "What does the task require? Who is the reader? What details must stay?"
- "Write 2-3 notes before you ask AI anything."

### 0:47--1:02 | Core Task 1: Common Summary Challenge
**Format:** Individual with instructor circulation  
**Language skills:** Writing (prompting as goal articulation), reading, critical evaluation

All students use `practice-files/01-study-spaces-article.md`.

**Task:** Get AI to produce a short, useful summary for a new B1 student while keeping specific details accurate.

Instructor circulates:
- "What constraints did you name?"
- "Which detail did AI miss or distort?"
- "Did you ask it to restate the task before generating?"

At the midway pause:
- "Stop. Compare the output with the file. Where is the gap?"
- Students revise and try again.

### 1:02--1:15 | Core Task 2: Turn Notes into an Email
**Format:** Individual, then pair check  
**Language skills:** Writing, reading, audience awareness

Students move to `practice-files/02-club-message-notes.txt`.

**Task:** Turn rough notes into a short email for classmates. Keep the date, time, reply deadline, and final question.

**Fallback:** Students without live AI use Task 4's prepared outputs and decide which better meets the task.

Pair check:
- "What details had to stay?"
- "Did the tone fit classmates?"
- "Which prompt revision improved the result most?"

### 1:15--1:27 | Model Comparison / No-Credit Fallback
**Format:** Pair work  
**Language skills:** Speaking, comparing, evaluating

Use one of two routes:

- **Live comparison:** two partners compare outputs from the same task.
- **Prepared comparison:** partners read `prepared-outputs/04-output-a.md` and `prepared-outputs/04-output-b.md` against `prepared-outputs/04-prompt.md`.

Students identify:
1. one constraint each output met or missed;
2. which response better fits the task; and
3. what a stronger revised prompt would say.

### 1:27--1:35 | Class Decision Log Setup
**Format:** Whole class, collaborative

Use one common example from the room and fill in the Decision Log fields together.

Key teacher line:

> "Because we started from the same file, we can see more clearly that the difference is often in what we asked, what the tool paid attention to, and what we verified."

## Segment C: Decision Log + Goal Document + Session Review (1:50--2:45)

### 1:50--2:12 | Individual Decision Log Entry
**Format:** Individual writing  
**Language skills:** Writing (structured documentation, ~150-200 words)

Students write Entry 1 about Task 1 or Task 2 in the [Markdown template](../materials/decision-log.md). Save it as `decision-logs/decision-log.md` in their own course folder, not the public practice repo. Later entries go in the same file. Follow the [Decision Log workflow](../onboarding/decision-logs.md).

Prompt them to capture:
- the file/task they used;
- the first prompt vs. revised prompt;
- one important mismatch;
- what they verified in the source file; and
- their decision: accept, modify, or reject.

### 2:12--2:24 | Goal Document Update: Bridge Back to Personal Interest
**Format:** Guided writing, then pair discussion  
**Language skills:** Writing, speaking

**Prompt:** "Which shared task was easiest for you to explain clearly? Which task connects most to something you actually want to do with a computer? What changed in your thinking today?"

**Sentence frames:**
- "The clearest task for me was _____ because _____."
- "This connects to my own idea about _____ because _____."
- "I changed my mind about _____ because _____."
- "Today I learned that AI needs me to say _____ clearly."

The Goal Document keeps the personal thread alive even though the practice files were shared.

### 2:24--2:26 | Bridging Activity
**Format:** Individual, silent

"Look at your Decision Log. Pick one place where the output did not match the file or the task. Rehearse how you will explain that gap."

### 2:26--2:38 | Session Review (Speaking Practice)
**Format:** Individual speaking practice. Prefer AI voice; typed AI exchange read aloud or live partner/instructor role-play remain valid.

**Role-setting prompt:** "Speaking practice. I'm a B1 English student practising explaining my work. Ask me follow-up questions. If my explanation is vague, tell me what's unclear and ask me to be more specific. Don't do the explaining for me."

**This week's scenario:** "Describe one shared practice task: what the file asked for, what you expected, what AI gave you, and what you had to clarify."

AI role: specificity coach.

### 2:38--2:39 | Self-Check
**Format:** Individual, written

"Write one sentence: what did you have to clarify most?"

### 2:39--2:45 | Closing
**Format:** Whole class

- 1-2 volunteers share a useful mismatch.
- Name the learning: "The common repo helped us see the same pattern in many versions. The tool changes, but the communication problem stays the same."
- Preview Week 3: saving states, restoring earlier work, and protecting files.

---

## Why this variant is worth using

- **Common experience:** easier teacher guidance and easier comparison across the room
- **Lower AI-credit pressure:** short, bounded tasks and a zero-credit fallback
- **Cleaner verification:** small inspectable files make mismatch and checking visible
- **Preserved course philosophy:** students still articulate, evaluate, revise, and reflect

## What this variant should not become

- not a permanent replacement for student-generated goals;
- not a hidden programming prerequisite;
- not a high-volume agent/CLI exercise; and
- not a one-tool requirement.
