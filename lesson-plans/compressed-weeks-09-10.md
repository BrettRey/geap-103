# GEAP 103 compressed lesson plan: Weeks 9 and 10

**Use with:** Any 12- or 13-content-meeting delivery  
**Context:** 3 × 55 minutes (150 minutes instruction + 15-minute break), Foundation Semester (B1)  
**Replaces:** `week-09.md` and `week-10.md` as two separate meetings

This is a redesigned meeting, not two complete lesson plans delivered at double speed. It preserves the Week 9 build/peer-explanation cycle and the Week 10 verification, documentation, reflection, and submission requirements. Project-type demonstrations are selective, and the Mid-Semester Artifact Package receives an access-neutral extension after class.

## Learning objectives

Students will:

1. develop an existing artifact in response to a concrete task list;
2. explain the gap between intention and draft to a peer;
3. verify the artifact and document what AI did, what the student changed, and what remains incomplete;
4. submit the Mid-Semester Artifact Package; and
5. identify interests and possible collaborators for Week 11.

## Before this meeting

At the end of Week 8, every student completes Goal Document entry 9:

> Choose an artifact you already started in this course. What specifically will you improve? Write three tasks. Put them in order. What must you finish before you can begin the next task?

Students should bring the selected artifact, but lack of a draft is not a reason to exclude them. Prepare a small set of starter artifacts from Weeks 5–8 that a student may adopt and improve.

Prepare:

- one short demonstration or guide for each project type actually represented in the class;
- an accessible starter artifact in at least document, data, and presentation/web formats;
- the process-documentation table and verification-note template;
- a way to save work that does not depend on GitHub or Copilot; and
- interest-inventory cards or a shared document.

Return Decision Log feedback round 2 in this meeting so students have a full interval to apply it before Week 11. If an exceptional delay makes that impossible, return it digitally within 24 hours and adjust the Decision Log deadline so students don't lose that response time.

## Segment A: readiness, language, and selective models (0:00–0:40)

### 0:00–0:05 | Readiness triage

Students show one of three states:

- **Ready:** artifact and task list open;
- **Artifact only:** write the three-task list now; or
- **No usable artifact:** choose a starter artifact and state one genuine audience or purpose for it.

Do not spend whole-class time repairing individual accounts. Route the student to an available local, browser, campus, or static-output path and begin the learning task.

### 0:05–0:17 | Vocabulary in one contrastive task

Use the Week 9 terms: **design, draft, evaluate, requirement, focus**.

Display a vague plan:

> Make my project better. Add some things. Fix the design.

Pairs rewrite it as three ordered tasks, using at least three target words. Collect two examples. This replaces five separate definitions while keeping productive use.

### 0:17–0:32 | Selective demonstrations

Demonstrate one common class need for no more than seven minutes. Students then open the guide or starter artifact matching their own project type. The demonstration answers:

1. What was the requirement?
2. What changed?
3. How was the change checked?

Do not demonstrate every application to the whole class. A student with a different tool follows the same requirement → change → check structure.

### 0:32–0:40 | Build priorities

Students open Goal Document entry 9 and mark:

- one task that must be completed today;
- one task that can be reduced; and
- one task that may be omitted and documented honestly.

## Segment B: build and peer evidence (0:40–1:35)

### 0:40–1:10 | Individual building

Students build using their available tool. They add one row to the process-documentation table after each substantial change:

| What I wanted | What the tool/AI did | What I changed | How I checked |
|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |

Circulation questions:

- “Which task are you on?”
- “What did you expect to happen?”
- “What is actually there?”
- “What can you submit honestly if this feature remains unfinished?”

### 1:10–1:28 | Two-way peer check-in

Pairs use six minutes each, then three minutes to save evidence.

**Presenter:** Show the draft and explain:

1. what you intended;
2. what the draft currently does;
3. the most important gap; and
4. one decision you made.

**Partner/client:** Ask one genuine question and give one specific response:

> “I understood _____. I am not sure about _____. Does your requirement mean _____?”

Each student saves a short note or screenshot showing that the exchange happened. This is the package's sharing evidence. If recording or screenshots are inaccessible, both students sign a dated two-sentence peer-check note.

### 1:28–1:35 | Save a recoverable state

Students save a version using GitHub, local Git, OneDrive/version history, or another declared path. The save message describes the completed work, not “updated file.”

## Break (1:35–1:50)

## Segment C: verify, document, reflect, and form groups (1:50–2:45)

### 1:50–2:00 | Verification model

Show a draft next to its stated requirement. Model a verification note with four parts:

> I checked _____. I found _____. I fixed _____. I did not finish _____ because _____.

Use the appropriate checker or test for the artifact. If no automated checker applies, test the artifact with a concrete sample and compare the result with the stated requirement.

### 2:00–2:20 | Verification and process documentation

Students:

1. test or inspect the artifact;
2. fix one issue if possible;
3. complete the process table in their own words; and
4. write the four-part verification note.

The artifact may be incomplete. Honest documentation is required; a falsely “perfect” package is not.

### 2:20–2:32 | Goal Document entry 10

> Compare what you planned with what you built. What is the biggest gap, and why did it happen? If you had more time, what would you change?

Optional frames:

- “I set out to build _____. What I actually built is _____.”
- “The biggest difference is _____ because _____.”
- “Although I planned to _____, I _____.”

### 2:32–2:42 | Collaboration interest inventory

Each student writes:

- “I am interested in _____.”
- “I can contribute _____.”
- “I need help with _____.”

Students identify two possible collaborators. Do not finalize every group under time pressure. Complete grouping during the first 10–15 minutes of Week 11, which also protects students absent today.

### 2:42–2:45 | Submission and closing

Display the package checklist:

- artifact;
- process documentation in the student's own words;
- verification note;
- peer-sharing evidence.

Use the section schedule's exact deadline. Students lacking dependable home access receive campus-computer time or an in-class submission route rather than an extension that presupposes a personal device.

## Non-happy-path branches

| What happens | Instructor response | Evidence preserved |
|---|---|---|
| Student has no draft | Start from a Week 5–8 artifact or provided starter; name a real audience and requirement | Task list, changes, verification, reflection |
| AI is unavailable or quota is exhausted | Use existing/static outputs or work directly in the available application | Student still evaluates, changes, checks, and documents |
| GitHub or cloud sync is unavailable | Save locally with a dated copy; add to version history later | Recoverable state plus accurate save note |
| Student missed Week 8 | Use a starter artifact and complete the Week 8 assessment make-up separately | Do not make this class carry two full assessments |
| Partner is absent | Make a triad or pair with another unmatched student; instructor is the final fallback audience | Individual peer-check note |
| Artifact will not be complete | Reduce scope; submit a functional partial result and name what remains | Honest verification note |
| Student cannot work after class | Protect the last 20 minutes for packaging and arrange campus-lab submission time | Access-neutral deadline path |
| Network fails for the class | Continue with local/starter files and paper process tables; upload later | All language and verification work continues |

## Assessment alignment

- Mid-Semester Artifact Package (8%): same four components and rubric as the standard Week 10 plan.
- Decision Logs: feedback round 2 must be returned before Week 11; the final eight-entry curation remains due in Week 11.
- Goal Document: entry 9 is completed before this meeting; entry 10 is completed in class.
- Speaking: the two-way peer check combines the Week 9 gap explanation and Week 10 client-audience demand.
- Collaboration: interest inventory begins group formation; final grouping occurs in Week 11.
