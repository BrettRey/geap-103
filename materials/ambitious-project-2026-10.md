# How should we work for the rest of the term?

GEAP 103 | October 2026

We're moving through the tasks slowly, and the project at the end may feel far away. I'd like your ideas.

Use your agent to read the course plan and the project ideas, talk about them with you, and help you tell me how you'd like to work. Your ideas can follow the course plan or be different. Please aim high.

**Send me your ideas by Friday, October 16, at 11:59 p.m.** (step 4)

Some things are the same for every GEAP 103 class: the seven course skills and the four parts of your grade. Almost everything else is open: what you make, how big it is, who you work with, and how we use class time.

## 1. Find out what the course plans

> Read the GEAP 103 course files at https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103. Start with README.md, topic-sequence.md, CLOs.md, materials/assessment-announcement-2026-10.md, my section's schedule in schedules/, and the student guide. Don't use archive/ or reviews/; they're old plans. In simple English, tell me what this course wants students to do and make, and what the final project is for. For each point, name the file it comes from.

Check one point yourself. Open the file. Is it really there?

## 2. Look at the project ideas

There are 26 [project ideas](https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/blob/main/admin/sample-project-ideas.md): apps, games, data tools, and creative and community projects.

> Read the GEAP 103 project ideas at https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/blob/main/admin/sample-project-ideas.md. Ask me three questions about my life, work and interests. Then suggest three ideas that fit me, from the list or new ones. For each idea, tell me what would make it hard to describe.

## 3. Talk it over with your agent

Ask about the course and about your own idea:

1. How could we work in class so that I can make something real by December?
2. If my idea works well, what could happen after it's finished? Help me choose a bigger goal.
3. What would an expert do here that I'm not asking for? What could show it works, other than you or me?
4. What other kinds of people would help make this great? What would each of them do?

Find one suggestion you disagree with. Why do you disagree?

## 4. Send me your ideas by Friday

Your agent can post your ideas as a GitHub issue. Tell it your answers first, then ask:

> Post a new issue at https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/issues with the title "Ideas: Monday" (or "Ideas: Wednesday"). Use these headings with my answers: What the course plans. How I want to work for the rest of the term. My ideas for the class. A project idea I like. What slows me down most. Alone, with a partner, or in a small group. Show me the issue before you post it. Don't include my name or student number.

You can also fill in the [ideas form](https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/issues/new?template=course-feedback.yml) yourself. You need a GitHub account for both.

Issues are public. Don't write your full name, student number or anything private. If you'd rather not post in public, or don't have a GitHub account, send me the same answers in a Blackboard message.

## 5. If you choose a project

Bring three things to your next conference:

1. Your goal in one sentence.
2. A first part you can show.
3. Who or what will check that it works.

Good goals name a real user and a date:

- "By December, a small family business will take orders through a web page I made."
- "A two-minute animated video that shows a parent how to set up a new phone. Three classmates will follow it and tell me where they got stuck."
- "A study planner that reminds me of every GEAP 103 deadline. I'll use it for four weeks and fix what doesn't work."

It's OK if not everything works. A failed attempt can still show learning and judgment. You need to show and explain what you did.

*Questions 2 to 4 in step 3 come from Felix Rieseberg, "How to be more ambitious: A tutorial for humans with models" (felixrieseberg.com).*
