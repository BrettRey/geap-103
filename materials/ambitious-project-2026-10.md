# Your project: aim high

GEAP 103 | October 2026

You've practised with Tasks 1 to 4. Now choose a project, and make it ambitious. Aim high, and be ready to show and explain each part yourself.

Your project becomes your **final package**: the product, short instructions, what works and what doesn't, and a note in your own words about how you made it. See [How you'll be assessed](assessment-announcement-2026-10.md).

**What stays the same:** the seven course skills, conferences, the four parts of your grade, and how you pass.

**What you choose:** what to build, how big, alone or with others, and which tasks you use. Go back to a task when your project needs that skill.

**Please send me your feedback (step 5) by Friday, October 16, at 11:59 p.m.**

## 1. Look at the project ideas

There are 26 [project ideas](https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/blob/main/admin/sample-project-ideas.md): apps, games, data tools, and creative and community projects. You don't have to choose one of them. Read them yourself, or ask your agent to read them with you:

> Read the GEAP 103 project ideas at https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/blob/main/admin/sample-project-ideas.md. Ask me three questions about my life, work and interests. Then suggest three ideas that fit me, from the list or new ones. For each idea, tell me what would make it hard to describe.

Talk about the ideas with your agent. Which one would you like to use, or show to someone?

## 2. Ask your agent to read the course

> Read these GEAP 103 files at https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103: the student guide, materials/assessment-announcement-2026-10.md, my section's schedule in schedules/, and CLOs.md. Don't use archive/, reviews/, design-notes/, lesson-plans/, or admin/ except the project ideas; they're old plans and teacher notes. In simple English, tell me what I need to make and show by the end of the course. For each point, name the file it comes from.

## 3. Ask three questions about your idea

Tell your agent what you'd like to make, even if the idea is small. Then ask:

1. If this works well, what could happen after it's finished? Help me choose a bigger goal.
2. What would an expert do here that I'm not asking for? What could show it works, other than you or me?
3. What other kinds of people would help make this great? What would each of them do?

## 4. Check what your agent said

- Open one file your agent named. Is the point really there?
- Find one suggestion you disagree with. Why do you disagree?

## 5. Tell me what you think, by Friday

Your agent can post your feedback for you as a GitHub issue. Tell it your answers first, then ask:

> Post a new issue at https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/issues with the title "Feedback: Monday" (or "Feedback: Wednesday"). Use these headings with my answers: How I want to work for the rest of the term (finish more tasks first, start a project now, a mix, or something else). Why. A project idea I like. What slows me down most. Alone, with a partner, or in a small group. One thing I'd change about the class. Show me the issue before you post it. Don't include my name or student number.

You can also fill in the [feedback form](https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/issues/new?template=course-feedback.yml) yourself.

Issues are public. Don't write your full name, student number or anything private. If you'd rather not post in public, send me the same answers in a Blackboard message.

## 6. Bring three things to your next conference

1. Your goal in one sentence.
2. A first part you can show.
3. Who or what will check that it works.

Good goals name a real user and a date:

- "By December, a small family business will take orders through a web page I made."
- "A two-minute animated video that shows a parent how to set up a new phone. Three classmates will follow it and tell me where they got stuck."
- "A study planner that reminds me of every GEAP 103 deadline. I'll use it for four weeks and fix what doesn't work."

It's OK if not everything works. A failed attempt can still show learning and judgment. You need to show and explain what you did.

*The three questions in step 3 come from Felix Rieseberg, "How to be more ambitious: A tutorial for humans with models" (felixrieseberg.com).*
