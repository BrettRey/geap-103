# How should we work for the rest of the term?

GEAP 103 | October 2026

We're moving through the tasks slowly, and the big project at the end may feel far away. Before we decide what to do next, I'd like your ideas.

Please do these three things with your agent, and send me your ideas by **Friday, October 16, at 11:59 p.m.**

## 1. Find out what the course imagines

> Read the GEAP 103 course files at https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103. Start with README.md, topic-sequence.md, CLOs.md, materials/assessment-announcement-2026-10.md, my section's schedule in schedules/, the student guide, and the project ideas in admin/sample-project-ideas.md. Don't use archive/ or reviews/; they're old plans. In simple English, tell me what this course imagines students will do and make by December. For each point, name the file it comes from.

Check one point yourself. Open the file. Is it really there?

## 2. Talk it over with your agent

Aim high. Ask your agent:

1. What's the most ambitious thing I could make by December, with your help? Use the project ideas as examples.
2. What would I need from our class to get there?
3. Should we follow the course plan, change it, or do something different? What's good and bad about each?

Find one suggestion you disagree with. Why do you disagree?

## 3. Tell me how you'd like to move forward

Your ideas can follow the course plan or be completely different. Your agent can post them as a GitHub issue. Tell it your answers first, then ask:

> Post a new issue at https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/issues with the title "Ideas: Monday" (or "Ideas: Wednesday"). Use these headings with my answers: What the course imagines. How I'd like us to work for the rest of the term, and why. The most ambitious thing I'd like to make. What I'd need from the class. Show me the issue before you post it. Don't include my name or student number.

You can also fill in the [ideas form](https://github.com/BrettRey/geap-103/issues/new?template=course-feedback.yml) yourself. You need a GitHub account for both.

Issues are public. Don't write your full name, student number or anything private. If you'd rather not post in public, or don't have a GitHub account, send me the same answers in a Blackboard message.
