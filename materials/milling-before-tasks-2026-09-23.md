# Ready to work with AI?

GEAP 103 | September 23, 2026 | Speaking warm-up

## Talk to your classmates

1. Choose a question. Think about your answer.
2. Find a partner. Ask your question and listen. Ask the follow-up question too.
3. Answer your partner's question. Then find a new partner.
4. Try to speak with at least four people. You don't need to finish all eight questions.

You can stay seated and let partners come to you. You can pass on a question. You don't need to write answers. Keep passwords, sign-in codes, and private information to yourself.

## Questions

### 1. Last week's success

Which part of setting up your GitHub account was easiest or hardest?

**Ask more:** What helped you finish?

### 2. Since our last class

Have you tried an AI tool since our last class?

**Ask more:** What did you try? If you haven't tried one, what would you like to try?

### 3. Where is my file?

Have you ever lost a file or had trouble finding one?

**Ask more:** What did you do next? If this hasn't happened to you, what would you try?

### 4. Before you send it

AI writes an email for you. What would you check before sending it?

**Ask more:** Why would you check that?

### 5. A clearer request

You ask AI, "Make this email better." What could you add to make your request clearer?

**Ask more:** How would you say the new request?

### 6. Before you say yes

AI wants to change several files. What would you want to know first?

**Ask more:** What question would you ask AI?

### 7. Asking for help

You're stuck on a computer task. What would you say to ask a classmate for help?

**Ask more:** What would your classmate need to know about the problem?

### 8. Today's goal

By the end of today, what would you like to be able to do?

**Ask more:** What could you show someone to demonstrate it?

## Useful phrases

- "For me, ..."
- "I'd try ... because ..."
- "Could you give an example?"
- "Do you mean ...?"
- "I'm not sure yet. What do you think?"

## Back together

Share one useful idea you heard. Then choose one thing you want to try today.

## Teacher notes (not on the student handout)

[Print the one-page student handout](../output/pdf/milling-before-tasks-2026-09-23.pdf).

Allow about 12 minutes: a minute to prepare, eight or nine minutes to mingle, and two minutes to share ideas. Print one handout per student or display the questions. Give different starting numbers so everyone doesn't begin with Question 1. Students can keep their question or choose a new one between partners. With an odd number, use one group of three.

This follows successful GitHub Education setup; it doesn't repeat account verification. Begin with experience and move towards the language students will use in the tasks: checking, clarifying, asking permission, and asking for help. Treat answers as ideas to explore, not a readiness test. Students needn't know Git terminology or have used a file-editing agent already.

During the closing discussion, invite two or three useful ideas. If needed, clarify that students should inspect what an action will change, keep useful work recoverable, and ask when they don't understand a permission request. Don't collect names or record conversations.

Transition: "Your accounts are ready. Now we'll use the task guide to make something, find the saved file, and check it. Start with Task 1. If you can already do a step, show it and move on."
